## Strategy
1. Search using the key entity names from your sub-question.
2. Read the most relevant chunks to find the answer.
3. VERIFY: Does the chunk actually mention the EXACT entity from your sub-question? Many chunks discuss entities with similar names (e.g., "Chris Summers" vs "Marc Summers", "Northern State Parkway" vs "Southern State Parkway"). If the chunk discusses a DIFFERENT entity, discard it and search again with more specific terms.
4. If your first search doesn't find relevant results, try different keywords or use semantic_search.
5. MANDATORY DOUBLE-CHECK: Before giving your answer, you MUST do one more keyword_search combining your candidate answer with the key entity to confirm the relationship. For example, if the sub-question is "Where was X born?" and you found "Paris", search "X Paris born" to confirm. If this search contradicts your answer, revise it.
6. Consider the original question — your answer should make sense as a step toward answering it. If the original question asks about a date, country, or person, make sure your answer leads toward that type of information.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Reading a chunk about entity A when the question asks about entity B (similar names)
- Accepting the first search result without reading the chunk carefully
- Giving a general answer (e.g., "India") when the question asks for something specific (e.g., a city in India)
- Stopping after one search — always try at least 2 searches with different keywords

## CRITICAL: Answer Format Rules
When you have found sufficient evidence, respond with ONLY the answer — a single entity name, date, number, or place.

CORRECT examples: "Steven Spielberg", "Rockland County", "December 14, 1814", "1986", "yes"
WRONG examples: "Lady Godiva was born in Coventry, England", "The answer is Steven Spielberg"

Just the entity. Nothing else. No sentences, no explanations, no prefixes.

If you cannot find the answer in the evidence, respond with just: unknown
NEVER explain why you could not find it. NEVER write sentences like "The evidence does not mention..." or "The provided documents do not contain...".
